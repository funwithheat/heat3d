Thermal processing

An HTST milk pasteurizer, running in real time

Cold raw milk is pre-warmed by milk that’s already been pasteurized, heated by hot water, then held for at least 15 seconds at 72 °C. If either number falls short, the diversion valve sends the milk back to start again.

Drying

A spray dryer turning milk into powder

Concentrated milk is flung into fine droplets inside a column of hot air. Each droplet dries in a couple of seconds as it falls. Too little heat or too much feed, and wet droplets hit the walls and stick.

Mechanical separation

A disc-stack centrifuge splitting cream from skim milk

Spinning at thousands of rpm, the bowl multiplies gravity several thousand times. Light fat globules drift inward to the cream outlet; denser skim milk is thrown outward. Small globules and cold, thick milk are what let fat escape.

Illustrative model: each globule drifts at a speed set by Stokes’ law, so size, spin and viscosity all matter.
